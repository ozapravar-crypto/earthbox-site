The Anatomy of an EarthBox Cradle

Deconstructing our signature holder design

By Aayush Lilani · 5 April 2026 · 4 min read

What makes the EarthBox cradle design unique? The cradle uses a parabolic curve that distributes weight evenly while creating visual lightness. The 3mm wall thickness provides strength without bulk. Integrated cable channels allow for hanging without visible hardware.

The cradle is our most refined piece—a holder that disappears, letting the plant and pot command attention. Every curve results from a decision. Here's what went into designing it.

The Problem with Plant Holders

Most plant holders announce themselves. Heavy materials, ornate details, visual competition with the plant they hold. We wanted the opposite—a form so quiet it becomes invisible, noticed only when you look for it.

Design constraints we set:

  • No visible screws or fasteners
  • Minimum material for maximum function
  • Works with multiple pot sizes without adjustment
  • Equally elegant from any viewing angle

The Parabolic Curve

The cradle's profile follows a parabola—the same curve that suspension bridges and satellite dishes use. Why? Physics.

A parabolic curve distributes load evenly along its length. Unlike a circular arc, which concentrates stress at certain points, a parabola handles weight uniformly. This means:

  • Less material needed (no thick stress reinforcement)
  • Consistent flex behavior (predictable, not brittle)
  • Visual continuity (no visible weak points)

"We didn't choose the parabola for aesthetics—we chose it for physics. That it also looks beautiful is a bonus of designing with, not against, natural forces."

Wall Thickness: 3mm Decision

The cradle walls are precisely 3mm thick. Thinner would flex too much under load; thicker would appear heavy and waste material. 3mm hits the optimum for:

  • Strength: Supports pots up to 2kg when wall-mounted
  • Flex: Slight give absorbs impact, preventing brittle failure
  • Print quality: Clean layers without sagging or gaps
  • Visual lightness: Thin enough to read as delicate
Testing Protocol

We tested wall thicknesses from 2mm to 5mm. 2mm failed under 1.5kg load. 4mm and above added weight without proportional strength gain. 3mm passed all load tests while maintaining intended aesthetics.

Hidden Cable Channels

Hanging a plant should not mean visible wires or chains breaking the visual line. The cradle integrates cable channels—internal pathways for suspension cords that exit only at designed points.

Channel design features:

  • 4mm diameter—fits standard hanging cord
  • Smooth internal finish—cord slides without catching
  • Entry/exit angles—prevent cord wear at transitions
  • Reinforced anchor points—stress concentration zones strengthened

The result: a hanging plant that appears to float, no visible hardware explaining the magic.

Pot Compatibility

The cradle accommodates pots from 8cm to 12cm diameter without adjustment. How? The parabolic profile creates a self-centering bowl shape—pots of different sizes simply rest at different depths.

This wasn't accidental. We modeled pot geometries and adjusted the curve until a single cradle design worked across our common size range. More specialized holders come in designated sizes, but the standard cradle serves most needs.

Material Choice for Cradles

Cradles are printed in ABS for indoor use, ASA for outdoor. The thin walls mean material properties matter more than in solid planters:

  • Impact resistance: Cradles get bumped; they need to flex, not crack
  • UV stability: Outdoor cradles see sun; ASA prevents yellowing
  • Consistency: Thin walls show print artifacts more; material must flow smoothly

Finishing Details

Print layer lines would destroy the cradle's intended elegance. Post-processing is essential:

  1. Support removal: Clean internal channels of all support material
  2. Sanding: Progressive grits (120 → 240 → 400) for smooth exterior
  3. Edge refinement: Sharp edges softened to comfortable touch
  4. Final inspection: 360° check under directional light

This finishing takes as long as printing. The printer creates the form; hands create the surface.

"A cradle should feel like it came from somewhere other than a machine. The print provides structure; finishing provides soul."

The Invisible Holder

Success for the cradle is when visitors ask about the plant, not the holder. When the eye goes to the terracotta, the green, the life—not the thing supporting it. Good design disappears into function.

Every decision in the cradle—the parabola, the 3mm walls, the hidden cables, the multi-size accommodation—serves this goal. The holder that does its job without announcing itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

What weight can the cradle hold?

Our standard cradle safely holds pots up to 2kg when wall-mounted. Floor cradles support up to 5kg.

Can the cradle be used outdoors?

Yes. We print outdoor cradles in ASA, which resists UV degradation. Specify outdoor use when ordering.
