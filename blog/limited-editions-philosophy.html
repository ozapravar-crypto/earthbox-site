Why We Release in Limited Editions

Scarcity as a design principle

By Aayush Lilani · 12 April 2026 · 5 min read

Every EarthBox piece is released in limited quantities—sometimes as few as twelve units, rarely more than fifty. This isn't a marketing gimmick to manufacture scarcity. It's a fundamental business decision that shapes everything from our production process to our relationship with customers. Here's why we work this way, and why we believe it produces better objects.

The Economics of Small Batches

Mass production rewards standardisation. The more identical units you make, the lower your cost per unit. This drives manufacturers toward the safest, most broadly appealing designs—the common denominator that offends no one but delights no one either.

Working in small batches inverts this logic. We're not optimising for cost per unit; we're optimising for quality per unit. This gives us freedom that large-scale producers can't afford:

  • Material experiments. We can try a new bioplastic formulation on a run of fifteen pieces. If it doesn't work, we've learned something valuable without massive waste.
  • Design risks. That unusual proportion that might not appeal to everyone? We can make it, find the people it resonates with, and move on.
  • Quality control. With twelve pieces, every single one can be inspected personally. With twelve thousand, you're relying on statistical sampling.

The Math

Our rejection rate is roughly 15%—pieces that don't meet our standards never leave the studio. At mass scale, that waste would be unsustainable. At our scale, it's the cost of quality.

Against Artificial Scarcity

We want to be clear: our limited editions aren't about creating hype or FOMO. We don't announce release dates on social media to drive frenzied purchasing. We don't artificially constrain supply to inflate prices. That approach feels manipulative, and it attracts customers more interested in exclusivity than in the objects themselves.

Our limits are genuine constraints:

Studio capacity. We're a small team with finite time. A single Specimen terrarium takes approximately four hours of hands-on work. Multiply that by our available hours, and you get a maximum output—currently around 40-60 terrariums per month across all designs.

Material availability. The moss varieties we prefer are wild-harvested sustainably, which means limited supply. The cork we source comes from a specific Portuguese producer who shares our values. These aren't infinitely scalable inputs.

Quality threshold. Every piece goes through our hands multiple times. When volume increases past a certain point, quality inevitably suffers. We've found our ceiling and we respect it.

What Edition Numbers Mean

Each EarthBox piece carries an edition number on its specimen card: "7 of 24" or "15 of 36." This number isn't arbitrary. It represents a specific production run with specific characteristics:

Same material batch. All pieces in an edition share the same filament spool, the same cork supplier, the same substrate mix. Tiny variations between batches mean pieces from different editions might have subtle differences—a slightly warmer white, a marginally different texture.

Same time period. An edition represents a window of focused production, usually one to two weeks. The maker's attention is undivided. This consistency shows in the finished work.

Complete documentation. We record which printer, which settings, which ambient conditions produced each edition. If something goes wrong—or exceptionally right—we can trace why.

The Relationship with Collectors

Over time, we've developed relationships with customers who've purchased multiple pieces. They understand our approach and trust it. They know that when we release something, it represents our best work at that moment—not a rushed product to hit a sales target.

"I've bought from EarthBox three times now. Each piece feels like it was made for me specifically, even though I know it wasn't." — A collector in Bangalore

This trust is earned slowly and easily lost. It requires us to maintain standards even when no one would notice a shortcut. The edition system keeps us honest—each piece is numbered, documented, accountable.

For Collectors

If you've missed an edition that's sold out, let us know. We often revisit successful designs in future editions, sometimes with subtle refinements.

Environmental Considerations

The environmental case for limited editions is straightforward: we never overproduce. We never have warehouses of unsold inventory that eventually gets discounted, donated, or destroyed. Every piece we make is made for someone who wants it.

This is the opposite of how most consumer goods work. Brands routinely manufacture far more than they sell, counting on the margin from sold units to cover the loss from unsold ones. The environmental cost of this overproduction is staggering and largely invisible to consumers.

Our model requires patience. When an edition sells out, it's gone until we produce again. Some customers find this frustrating. But we'd rather ask for patience than participate in the overproduction cycle that's choking our planet.

The Future of Limited Editions

We're sometimes asked whether we'll scale up—expand the studio, hire more makers, increase production. It's possible, but only under conditions that preserve what makes our work meaningful:

  • More makers would need to share our values and meet our standards
  • Growth would need to be gradual enough to maintain quality
  • Each edition would still need individual attention

For now, we're content with our constraints. They force creativity, maintain quality, and keep our relationship with customers personal. Not every business model needs to scale infinitely. Some things work better small.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will sold-out designs return?

No. When an edition sells out, it closes permanently. This ensures each piece remains special.

Can I request a custom version of old designs?

Occasionally. Contact us to discuss—custom commissions are considered case by case.
