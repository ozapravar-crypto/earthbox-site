The Specimen Card: Documenting Each Piece

Provenance and the paper record

By Aayush Lilani · 8 April 2026 · 4 min read

Every EarthBox terrarium ships with a specimen card—a printed document that might seem like elaborate packaging but serves a deeper purpose. These cards are part provenance record, part care guide, part philosophical statement. Here's why we invest significant effort in what many companies would consider an afterthought.

The Problem with Anonymous Objects

Most products arrive stripped of context. You know where you bought it, maybe where it was made (usually a country, rarely more specific), and that's it. The object exists in isolation, disconnected from its origin, its makers, its materials.

This anonymity serves mass production—it allows products to be churned out without accountability, interchangeable units from a faceless supply chain. But it impoverishes the relationship between objects and their owners. How can you feel connected to something you know nothing about?

Consider This

You likely know more about the coffee you drink (origin, roast date, tasting notes) than about most objects in your home. We think that's backwards.

What the Specimen Card Contains

Each card is printed on heavy cotton stock and includes:

Unique identifier. Every terrarium has a code (like SP-2024-037) that links to our internal records. If you ever need to contact us about your piece, this number tells us exactly what you have.

Edition information. "Number 7 of 24" tells you exactly where your piece sits within its production run. Not a serial number generated by software, but a position in a specific batch made during a specific period.

Creation date. The actual date—not month, not quarter—when your terrarium was assembled. This establishes the starting point for the ecosystem's life and helps track its age.

Plant inventory. Every species included, with both common and scientific names. "Fittonia albivenis 'White Anne'" tells you exactly what you're caring for, not just "nerve plant."

Substrate composition. The specific mix of materials forming the foundation layer. Knowing this helps if you ever need to refresh the substrate or troubleshoot drainage issues.

Vessel specifications. Dimensions, material (which PLA formulation, from which supplier), print settings. Complete transparency about what the object is made of.

Care instructions. Condensed guidance specific to this exact configuration of plants and vessel, not generic terrarium advice.

Provenance in Craft Traditions

Documentation travelling with objects has deep roots in craft traditions. Japanese pottery often includes certificates from the maker. Fine furniture carries maker's marks. Vintage wines have labels tracing vintage, vineyard, and bottling. These documents transform anonymous products into known objects with traceable histories.

"Objects without documentation are orphans. They exist, but no one knows where they came from or who made them."

We're applying this traditional approach to a contemporary context. 3D printing is a new fabrication method, but the values of documentation and accountability are timeless.

Practical Benefits of Documentation

Beyond philosophy, specimen cards provide real utility:

Care accuracy. Generic terrarium advice often contradicts because it tries to cover all cases. Your card tells you exactly what your specific plants need—this Fittonia variety prefers slightly more moisture than others, this moss species tolerates lower light.

Troubleshooting support. When customers contact us with questions, the specimen code lets us pull up complete records. We can see the exact configuration, check notes from assembly, and provide specific guidance rather than generic suggestions.

A Recent Example

A customer reported unusual condensation. Her specimen code revealed she had our experimental high-humidity configuration. We could immediately suggest the specific venting schedule for that setup.

Replacement parts. If a vessel needs replacing years later (unlikely but possible), your documentation tells us exactly what to fabricate. Same dimensions, same material, same finish.

Resale value. Should you ever part with your terrarium, complete documentation significantly increases its value to collectors. The object comes with its full story.

The Making of a Specimen Card

Creating the cards is itself a careful process:

Paper selection. We use 300gsm cotton stock with a subtle texture. It feels substantial in hand and ages gracefully. Cheaper card stock yellows; cotton paper develops character.

Printing method. Core text is digitally printed for legibility. Edition numbers are hand-stamped using brass type—a deliberate inefficiency that makes each card unique.

Signing. Each card is signed by the maker who assembled that specific terrarium. This isn't ceremonial—it establishes personal accountability. If something's wrong with piece #37, we know exactly who to ask.

Envelope packaging. Cards ship in glassine envelopes to protect them during transit. They're designed to be stored with important documents or displayed alongside the terrarium.

A Living Document

We encourage customers to treat specimen cards as living documents. Make notes about your terrarium's evolution. Record when you first vented it, when you noticed new growth, when you added or removed plants. The card becomes a diary of your ecosystem's life.

Some customers have sent us photos of their annotated cards—years of observations recorded in margins and on the back. These documents tell richer stories than we could ever write ourselves. The specimen card provides the foundation; the owner writes the rest.

In a disposable age, we're making objects meant to last and documents meant to travel with them. The specimen card is a small gesture toward permanence—a promise that this object has a known origin and deserves to have its story told.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why include paper documentation?

Digital records disappear. A physical card stays with the object, connecting future owners to its origin.

What information is on the specimen card?

Edition number (e.g., 23/50), production date, materials, dimensions, care instructions, and maker signature.
