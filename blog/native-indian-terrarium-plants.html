5 Native Indian Plants Perfect for Terrariums

Local species that thrive in glass

By Aayush Lilani · 22 April 2026 · 7 min read

Which Indian plants grow well in terrariums? Native spikemosses (Selaginella), Peperomia tetraphylla from the Western Ghats, maidenhair ferns (Adiantum), local mosses and brake ferns (Pteris) all suit terrarium conditions. Pilea cadierei and Ficus pumila are popular here too, but they are not native.

Why import plants from Southeast Asian nurseries when India's own forests contain perfect terrarium candidates? Native Indian plants often settle in faster, need less adjustment, and connect your terrarium to local ecology. Here are five native plants that do well in closed glass environments, most of which you can find in Mumbai nurseries.

1. Selaginella (Indian Spikemoss)

Several Selaginella species grow wild in India, including in the Western Ghats—the same mountains visible from Mumbai on clear days. The best known is Selaginella bryopteris, known as sanjeevani. These feathery, prehistoric-looking plants thrive in terrarium humidity.

Why it works:

  • Native species already know India's heat and monsoon
  • Tolerates low light (forest floor plant)
  • Spreads to fill spaces naturally
  • Indicates moisture levels (curls when dry)

Care notes:

  • Keep consistently moist—most species brown if left to dry
  • Avoid direct sunlight
  • Trim if it becomes too vigorous

2. Peperomia Species

Several Peperomia species are native to India, including species from the Western Ghats and Northeastern states. These compact plants with interesting foliage are terrarium staples worldwide.

Species to know:

  • Peperomia tetraphylla: Native. Small leaves in clusters of four, compact; grows on mossy trees and rocks in the Western Ghats
  • Peperomia pellucida: Not native—it comes from the tropical Americas—but it has naturalised across India as a monsoon weed. Translucent stems, heart-shaped leaves

Care notes:

  • Moderate humidity—adapts to terrarium range
  • Tolerates neglect better than most terrarium plants
  • Propagates easily from leaf cuttings

"People import exotic Peperomia when native species grow on the mossy trees of the Western Ghats. Local plants know local conditions."

3. Adiantum (Maidenhair Fern)

The delicate maidenhair fern—Adiantum species—grows wild throughout India, including in Sanjay Gandhi National Park within Mumbai's city limits. Its fine, fan-shaped leaves epitomize terrarium elegance.

Why it works:

  • Demands high humidity—terrariums provide exactly this
  • Graceful appearance belies surprising toughness
  • Multiple native species to choose from

Care notes:

  • Consistent moisture essential—never let dry completely
  • Avoid handling fronds (oils damage leaves)
  • Trim dead fronds at base to encourage new growth
Where to Find

Look for Adiantum at Dadar flower market during monsoon season. Also grows wild at the edges of Aarey Colony—observe rather than collect from protected areas.

4. Local Mosses

The mosses that turn Mumbai's walls and tree trunks green each monsoon are local plants. In a closed terrarium they carpet soil and stone, and their colour tells you how moist the system is.

Why it works:

  • Thrives in the humidity a closed terrarium holds
  • Needs little light and no feeding
  • Shows moisture at a glance—greens when moist, browns when dry

Care notes:

  • Press firmly onto moist soil so it makes contact
  • Avoid direct sunlight
  • Quarantine new moss for a week or two to check for pests
Sourcing Moss

Buy moss from a nursery, or collect only from your own garden or a space where you have permission. Never collect from Sanjay Gandhi National Park, Aarey or any other protected area.

5. Pteris (Brake Ferns)

Brake ferns are common in Indian nurseries, and Pteris cretica grows wild in India's hill forests. Compact forms with white-striped fronds suit terrariums.

Why it works:

  • Enjoys the steady humidity of a closed terrarium
  • Tolerates the lower light of an indoor shelf
  • Adds height and a different leaf shape to moss and spikemoss

Care notes:

  • Keep the soil moist but not waterlogged
  • Trim old fronds at the base
  • Best in medium to large containers—it can outgrow small jars
Popular, but Not Native

Two plants often sold as local terrarium plants are not native to India. Pilea cadierei (aluminum plant) comes from Vietnam and China. Ficus pumila (creeping fig) comes from East Asia, and it is not the "Indian fig". Both are widely grown here and do well in terrariums—just choose them knowing where they're from.

Where to Source Native Plants

Finding native terrarium plants in Mumbai:

  • Dadar flower market: Best selection during monsoon
  • Matunga nurseries: Several shops specialize in foliage plants
  • Versova nurseries: Good for ferns and moisture-loving species
  • Online plant nurseries: Many ship to Mumbai

When purchasing, look for:

  • Plants grown in humid greenhouse conditions (pre-adapted)
  • No signs of pests or disease
  • Compact, proportional growth (not leggy)

The Case for Native Plants

Imported plants look exotic but can struggle with local conditions. Native species know Mumbai's heat, humidity patterns, and light quality. They need less adjustment, show less stress, and connect your terrarium to the ecology outside your window.

Every terrarium containing native plants is a small conservation act—appreciation of Indian biodiversity, support for local nurseries, and proof that beauty doesn't require importation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I buy native terrarium plants in Mumbai?

Visit nurseries in Dadar flower market, Matunga, or Versova. Local sellers often have Selaginella and ferns; ask which species, since many nursery spikemosses are not native.

Are imported terrarium plants better?

Not necessarily. Native plants often adapt faster and need less adjustment. They also support local biodiversity.
