5 Native Indian Plants Perfect for Terrariums

Local species that thrive in glass

By Aayush Lilani · 22 April 2026 · 7 min read

Which Indian plants grow well in terrariums? Selaginella (Indian spikemoss), Peperomia species native to Western Ghats, Pilea (aluminum plant), native ferns like Adiantum, and Indian fig (Ficus pumila) all thrive in terrarium conditions and are available locally.

Why import plants from Southeast Asian nurseries when India's own forests contain perfect terrarium candidates? Native Indian plants adapt faster, need less adjustment, and connect your terrarium to local ecology. Here are five species—all available in Mumbai—that excel in closed glass environments.

1. Selaginella (Indian Spikemoss)

Multiple Selaginella species grow wild in India's Western Ghats—the same mountains visible from Mumbai on clear days. These feathery, prehistoric-looking plants thrive in terrarium humidity.

Why it works:

  • Native to humid Indian forests—no adjustment period
  • Tolerates low light (forest floor plant)
  • Spreads to fill spaces naturally
  • Indicates moisture levels (curls when dry)

Care notes:

  • Keep consistently moist—doesn't tolerate drying
  • Avoid direct sunlight
  • Trim if it becomes too vigorous

2. Peperomia Species

Several Peperomia species are native to India, including varieties from the Western Ghats and Northeastern states. These compact plants with interesting foliage are terrarium staples worldwide—and some of the best are Indian.

Recommended species:

  • Peperomia pellucida: Translucent stems, heart-shaped leaves
  • Peperomia reflexa: Trailing habit, small leaves
  • Peperomia tetraphylla: Four-leaf clusters, compact

Care notes:

  • Moderate humidity—adapts to terrarium range
  • Tolerates neglect better than most terrarium plants
  • Propagates easily from leaf cuttings

"People import exotic Peperomia when native species grow as weeds in Matheran. Local plants know local conditions."

3. Adiantum (Maidenhair Fern)

The delicate maidenhair fern—Adiantum species—grows wild throughout India, including in Sanjay Gandhi National Park within Mumbai's city limits. Its fine, fan-shaped leaves epitomize terrarium elegance.

Why it works:

  • Demands high humidity—terrariums provide exactly this
  • Graceful appearance belies surprising toughness
  • Multiple native species to choose from

Care notes:

  • Consistent moisture essential—never let dry completely
  • Avoid handling fronds (oils damage leaves)
  • Trim dead fronds at base to encourage new growth
Where to Find

Look for Adiantum at Dadar flower market during monsoon season. Also grows wild at the edges of Aarey Colony—observe rather than collect from protected areas.

4. Pilea (Aluminum Plant)

Pilea cadierei—called aluminum plant for its silvery leaf markings—has naturalized in parts of India. It combines ease of care with striking visual interest.

Why it works:

  • Tolerates terrarium humidity without issues
  • Compact growth suits small containers
  • Silver markings add visual contrast to green companions
  • Readily available in Mumbai nurseries

Care notes:

  • Pinch growing tips to encourage bushiness
  • Appreciates slightly more light than ferns
  • Propagates easily from stem cuttings

5. Ficus pumila (Indian Fig)

The creeping fig—Ficus pumila—climbs walls throughout Indian cities. In a terrarium, it provides living architecture, climbing backgrounds and covering surfaces.

Why it works:

  • Vigorous grower—establishes quickly
  • Creates vertical interest other plants can't
  • Native adaptation to Indian humidity patterns

Care notes:

  • Requires regular trimming—grows fast
  • Can dominate smaller terrariums if unchecked
  • Works best in larger containers with vertical elements

"Ficus pumila grows on every other building in Mumbai. It knows this climate. In a terrarium, it performs even better with consistent humidity."

Where to Source Native Plants

Finding native terrarium plants in Mumbai:

  • Dadar flower market: Best selection during monsoon
  • Matunga nurseries: Several shops specialize in foliage plants
  • Versova nurseries: Good for ferns and moisture-loving species
  • Online: Ugaoo and Nurserylive ship to Mumbai

When purchasing, look for:

  • Plants grown in humid greenhouse conditions (pre-adapted)
  • No signs of pests or disease
  • Compact, proportional growth (not leggy)

The Case for Native Plants

Imported plants look exotic but often struggle with local conditions. Native species know Mumbai's heat, humidity patterns, and light quality. They need less adjustment, show less stress, and connect your terrarium to the ecology outside your window.

Every terrarium containing native plants is a small conservation act—appreciation of Indian biodiversity, support for local nurseries, and proof that beauty doesn't require importation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I buy native terrarium plants in Mumbai?

Visit nurseries in Dadar flower market, Matunga, or Versova. Local sellers often have Selaginella and native ferns.

Are imported terrarium plants better?

Not necessarily. Native plants adapt faster and require less adjustment. They also support local biodiversity.
